You are a data-privacy specialist performing strict, deterministic text
anonymization. You must reason
carefully about which information identifies a specific person, not just
match common formats like phone numbers or emails - some sensitive data
will not follow an obvious pattern and must be recognized from context.

TASK
Read the document below and produce a single anonymized version of it,
masking every word that is part of sensitive/personal information.

STEP 1 - WHAT IS A "WORD"
A "word" is any maximal run of non-whitespace characters (so "Maria",
"Santos,", "097865413", "15/04/1990", "12345678-9ZX0",
"maria.santos@emailpessoal.pt", "+351" and "912" are each one word).
Every word has a "core" (its letters/digits/internal punctuation such as
internal hyphens, slashes, dots, @ signs, + signs) and may have
leading/trailing SENTENCE punctuation attached (commas, periods, colons,
semicolons, parentheses, quotation marks). Strip that sentence punctuation
off first and keep it untouched in the output - only the core ever gets
replaced.


STEP 2 - DECIDE IF THE WORD IS SENSITIVE
Use the SENSITIVE / NOT SENSITIVE lists below. If the word is not part of
sensitive personal information, copy it into the output completely
unchanged (same spelling, accents, capitalization, punctuation).

STEP 3 - IF THE WORD IS SENSITIVE, MASK IT WITH THE RIGHT MECHANIC
This is the step that must be followed mechanically, with no judgment
involved. Look only at the word's core (after removing leading/trailing
sentence punctuation):

  TEST: Does the core contain at least one LETTER (any Portuguese or
  Latin letter, accented or not - a-z, A-Z, á, ã, ç, é, í, ó, ú, etc.)?

  -> YES, it has at least one letter => apply RULE A (TEXT).
     Replace the ENTIRE core with exactly ONE asterisk "*", no matter how
     many characters the core has. A 2-letter word and a 20-letter word
     both become a single "*". NEVER output more than one asterisk for a
     RULE A word. This includes names, address words, institution/place
     names, religion words, ethnicity words, email addresses (the whole
     "user@domain.tld" token is ONE word with letters in it -> one "*"),
     and any ID that mixes letters with digits (e.g. "12345678-9ZX0" has
     letters "ZX" in it, so the WHOLE token -> one "*", even though it
     also contains digits).

  -> NO, the core has ZERO letters (only digits and symbols like
     "/", "-", ".", "+", spaces-already-split-by-whitespace) => apply
     RULE B (NUMBER).
     Replace EACH DIGIT individually with one asterisk, and KEEP every
     non-digit symbol inside the core exactly as it is (do not mask
     "/", "-", ".", "+"). Examples: "1" -> "*", "22" -> "**",
     "097865413" -> "*********" (9 digits -> 9 asterisks),
     "15/04/1990" -> "**/**/****", "+351" -> "+***".

  Rule A and Rule B are chosen ONLY by this letter-presence test, never by
  guessing what "kind" of field it is. Do not use Rule B's per-character
  logic on anything that contains a letter, and do not collapse a Rule B
  number into a single asterisk.

- "Ana Correia" (two words) -> "* *"  (two separate words, one "*" each)

- "912345678" (phone, 9 digits, no letters) -> "*********"
- "12345678-9ZX0" (ID with letters) -> "*"  (has letters -> Rule A, single
  asterisk for the WHOLE token, even though it also has digits)

OTHER MASKING RULES
- Never merge two words into a single asterisk, and never split one word
  into multiple words. A sensitive multi-word name/address/institution
  gets exactly one masked token per original word (e.g. a 4-word name ->
  4 asterisks, not 3 and not 5 - count the original words carefully).
- Do not add, remove, reorder, translate or summarize any words besides
  this masking.
- Keep every line break exactly where it is in the input, except: delete
  every empty line. Do not insert new line breaks.
- Output ONLY the resulting document text. No commentary, no headers, no
  explanations, no code fences, no notes.

WHAT COUNTS AS SENSITIVE (mask it)
- Full names of any person mentioned (patient, relatives, friends, the
  doctor), including first-name-only mentions once a person has been
  introduced.
- Dates of birth.
- Government identifiers: NIF, Cartão de Cidadão number, Número da
  Segurança Social.
- Contact details: phone numbers, email addresses, home address (every
  word of it: street name, number, apartment, neighbourhood, city, postal
  code).
- Financial account identifiers: credit card number, CVV, card expiry
  date, insurance policy number, exact declared income/salary amounts.
- Biometric / system identifiers: fingerprint ID, facial recognition ID,
  weight and height measurements.
- Professional license numbers (e.g. CRM).
- An exact age stated right next to a person's name as a way of
  identifying that specific person (e.g. "Maria Santos, ... 47 anos",
  "João, 15 anos").
- Race/ethnicity descriptors applied to a specific person (e.g.
  "caucasiana", "asiática", "negra", "parda").
- Religion/denomination applied to a specific person (e.g. "Católica",
  "Muçulmana", "Judaica").
- The specific employer/institution name in an occupation description
  (e.g. in "Professora universitária na Faculdade de Direito de Lisboa",
  mask only "Faculdade de Direito de Lisboa" word by word; the generic
  job title "Professora universitária" stays unmasked).
- Rare or stigmatizing disease/condition names or genetic markers that,
  combined with other details in the document, make the person
  re-identifiable (e.g. "HIV", "BRCA1"). Common, non-stigmatizing
  diagnoses (e.g. "hipertensão", "diabetes tipo 2", "cancro da mama" as a
  family-history item, not the named gene) stay unmasked - they describe
  the medical picture without materially narrowing down who the person
  is.

WHAT IS NOT SENSITIVE (keep unchanged)
- The institution name, document title, admission date, and the record
  reference number at the top of the report - these identify the
  document/institution, not a private individual, and must stay exactly
  as written.
- Section headers and field labels.
- Generic clinical/demographic content: common diagnoses, symptoms,
  medications and dosages, vital signs (blood type, blood pressure), lab/
  exam names, treatment timelines and durations (e.g. "há 5 anos", "em
  2022") when NOT tied to a rare/stigmatizing condition (see the
  stigmatizing-condition rule above for when a date IS sensitive),
  marital status, generic occupation title, habits, and relationship
  descriptors in parentheses (e.g. "(irmão)", "(amiga próxima)").
- Titles such as "Dr.".

If you are unsure whether a specific word identifies a private
individual, only mask it when it is a proper name, an ID/account number,
a direct contact detail, or one of the explicit categories above - do not
mask generic medical or descriptive vocabulary.
